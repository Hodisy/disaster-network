# Southern France flooding on 20 October 2026

Research snapshot: 22 October 2026. Scenario time zone: Europe/Paris.

Disaster Network can credibly use the 19 to 20 October Mediterranean rainfall episode as dated context for a fictional Allstorm Insurance portfolio. The strongest local evidence supports two different operational problems: flooded and isolated homes around Entressen, and evacuation or access restrictions associated with ground instability at Les Heures Claires in Istres. These situations require different assistance and inspection workflows.

There is no consolidated, deduplicated count of everyone affected in the sources established here, and no verified event-wide insured-loss total. People rescued, households evacuated, electricity customers disconnected and insurance claims are different measures. None can be substituted for another.

This report contains three explicitly separated layers: established public facts, insurance procedure, and proposed synthetic scenario assumptions. It does not change the application or establish actual coverage for any person.

# Insurance procedure and coverage

## Recognition is a separate administrative process

On 21 October, Istres said it intended to request CatNat recognition in the following days and asked residents for damage information. A request is not recognition. This research did not establish a published order recognising this particular episode for Istres. Use an unverified or pending status until the matching order, commune, dates and hazard have been checked. [Municipal CatNat notice](https://www.istres.fr/index.php?cHash=f508a2b5360ba4b2a87a27bb2671109d&id=229&tx_news_pi1%5Baction%5D=detail&tx_news_pi1%5Bcontroller%5D=News&tx_news_pi1%5Bnews%5D=13456)

The statutory CatNat route requires an applicable property-damage contract and a recognition order identifying the relevant commune, period and peril. Only insured property falls within the contract's scope. Motor damage and business interruption require their own applicable cover; a household policy is not a general guarantee for every loss. [Service Public](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F3076)

## Practical claim handling

1. Notify the insurer promptly, record the event date separately from the notification date, and request urgent assistance if needed.
2. Identify the policy, property, occupancy, declared damage and access restrictions.
3. Assemble photographs already available, an itemised loss inventory, invoices and other evidence of ownership or value. Do not request unsafe re-entry to obtain documents.
4. Separate assistance from the final assessment of damage and contractual cover.
5. Arrange assessment and authorised protective measures as appropriate, retaining evidence and receipts.
6. Obtain the coverage position and settlement proposal, then track agreement, payment or repair mobilisation separately from closure.

The evidence and insured-property requirements above follow [Service Public](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F3076); the workflow separation is a proposed application design.

## Deadlines and deductibles

For CatNat, notification is due as soon as the insured learns of the loss, no later than 30 days after publication of the recognition order. Article L125-2 also sets one month from declaration or later recognition to explain guarantee activation and order any necessary expertise; one month from the loss estimate without expertise or the final expert report to propose settlement; then 21 days to pay or one month to appoint the repair company after agreement. A provision is due within two months of the estimate or later recognition. [Code des assurances, L125-2, current version indexed from 28 May 2026](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000006792617)

These are trigger-dependent legal periods, not a promise to close every file immediately. Some public summaries still use a blanket three-month description; use the applicable legal text and policy version before implementing timers.

Géorisques describes a standard EUR 380 deductible for individual non-drought CatNat losses, with a possible lower contractual storm/hail/snow deductible under the applicable rules. Do not apply a universal EUR 380 deduction to every possible cover or insured item. [Géorisques deductible guidance](https://www.georisques.gouv.fr/minformer-sur-la-prevention-des-risques/la-garantie-cat-nat)

Istres also urged prompt notification within five days without waiting for recognition. This local advice does not replace the statutory CatNat rule. [City notice](https://www.istres.fr/index.php?cHash=f508a2b5360ba4b2a87a27bb2671109d&id=229&tx_news_pi1%5Baction%5D=detail&tx_news_pi1%5Bcontroller%5D=News&tx_news_pi1%5Bnews%5D=13456)

## A credible French insurer reference

MAIF's public guidance states that its home insurance can cover flooding without a CatNat order, within policy conditions. Its guidance also describes emergency measures and temporary accommodation. This supports the plausibility of contractual assistance before statutory recognition; it does not establish a specific household's entitlement, the version of its policy or a response to this event. [MAIF flood guidance](https://www.maif.fr/habitation/guide-assurance-habitation/inondation)

Use MAIF only as a public industry reference. Preserve fictional Allstorm Insurance branding, do not imply affiliation, and write clearly fictional Allstorm demonstration terms. Any future claim of actual MAIF coverage must use verified general and individual policy documents.

## Implications for insurer operations

The following are operational inferences from the documented situation, not an insurer's published loss estimate:

| Documented situation | Likely handling requirement | Data needed in Disaster Network |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ground-floor flooding | Assess building, contents, electrical inspection, cleaning and drying needs | Cause, damage, photos, utilities, inspection status |
| Evacuation with uncertain property damage | Establish safe accommodation needs while leaving damage and coverage unresolved | Access restriction, occupancy, accommodation, confirmation time |
| Ground instability | Specialist review before a routine repair crew is mobilised | Hazard and access gate, authority update, expert requirement |
| Road and utility disruption | Reschedule inspections and sequence work by actual access and utility status | Resource skills, capacity, access, latest update |
| Many concurrent files | Identify shared needs without confusing urgency with insured value | Explainable priority, unmet needs, claim stage, uncertainty |

Exposure estimates must separate reported damage, provisional covered loss, assistance budgets, authorised commitments and payments. No published cost total was established for the event, and public recovery aid is not an insured-loss total. Reinsurance recoveries depend on actual treaties and should not be fabricated.

# Proposed demonstration scenario

## Scope and date

Use Jules's fictional Allstorm household reporting on 22 October about damage originating on 20 October near Istres. Keep the event date, report date, source publication date and last update distinct. The wider map covers the Berre Lagoon area; synthetic markers do not define an official flood extent. The [80 household insurer brief](../scenarios/insurer-workspace-brief.md) is the reference for fixture counts, fictional coverage terms and Jules's case.

This retains the existing client scenario date. It also avoids presenting an ideal internet-connected call as if connectivity had been universal during the peak disruption on 20 October. A browser call remains a simulated product interaction, not a statement about access to networks in every affected street.

## Individual journey

Jules is a fictional owner-occupier living with his partner and one child. The household is safe with his sister but needs another accommodation solution. He reports ground-floor water damage and damaged furniture/appliances. His exact household, photographs, inventory and losses are invented. Do not attach invented damage to a named real resident or present a generated image as an event photograph.

The hotline creates a report, checks the demonstration contract and records that any CatNat recognition remains unverified. It asks for existing evidence and an inventory without requiring re-entry. The portal receives a synthetic invoice and already-available photographs. Document completeness advances; coverage assessment remains a separate stage. Site access is initially unknown; physical missions remain blocked until a clearly synthetic access update permits them.

The fictional **Allstorm Home Protect**, version 1, permits consideration of direct flood damage and specified assistance before recognition. Its limits are invented demonstration terms, not real policy coverage:

| Benefit | Fictional limit | Jules's initial estimate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emergency accommodation | 7 nights, EUR 150 per household/night, maximum EUR 1,050 | EUR 1,000 |
| Essential items | EUR 250 per household/event | EUR 250 |
| Electrical inspection and safety | EUR 500 | EUR 350 |
| Cleaning and drying | EUR 3,000 | EUR 1,800 |
| Moving and temporary storage | EUR 1,000 | EUR 600 |
| Damaged contents | EUR 30,000 | EUR 4,500 |
| Insured building repairs | EUR 200,000 | EUR 9,000 |
| Gross estimate | Not a benefit limit | EUR 17,500 |

Jules's estimate comprises EUR 4,000 assistance/protection and EUR 13,500 property damage. The fictional contractual flood route has one EUR 300 deductible on combined eligible contents/building, with no assistance deductible. If all lines were approved at the estimated amounts, the illustrative insurer total would be EUR 17,200. Initially this remains an estimate, with EUR 0 paid; it is neither an approved amount nor an actual settlement. The statutory CatNat route requires separate review and cannot be stacked to compensate the same cost twice.

Accommodation beyond seven nights, uncertain damage and ground instability require separate review. An evacuated household with no confirmed physical damage is not automatically eligible for building compensation. Full terms, conditions and the frontend handoff are in the [scenario brief](../scenarios/insurer-workspace-brief.md).

## Collective journey across 80 synthetic files

The fixture represents 80 households and 170 people. Istres's 2023 average is 2.15 occupants per main residence, which would give approximately 172 people across 80 residences. Our exact synthetic mix instead uses 30 single-person, 25 two-person, 10 three-person and 15 four-person households, totalling 170. This is a plausibility reference, not an estimate of the real affected population. [Insee household size](https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/2011101?geo=COM-13047)

The mutually exclusive situation groups are **15 uninhabitable homes with reported damage, 10 evacuated/inaccessible homes with unknown damage, 35 damaged but habitable homes, and 20 limited or unconfirmed cases**. Jules belongs to the first group and is included within the total, not added as an 81st case. A temporary stay with relatives does not remove a need for accommodation, but need and immediate placement demand are separate fields.

| Entressen | 20 | Flood damage, access and utility constraints |

| Other Istres locations | 15 | Mixed needs, including Jules |

These are not observed neighbourhood claim counts. Every household, resource, cost and classification remains synthetic. A file can have several needs, so need totals may exceed 80. Classification should distinguish reported damage, uninhabitability, evacuation, missing documents, coverage review and resource assignment. Compute exposure from individual cost lines rather than multiplying Jules's estimate by the portfolio size.

The replanning trigger can be the loss of availability of a fictional drying team. The agent proposes replacement assignments for accessible properties while keeping access-restricted files blocked. It does not override municipal access instructions. Any improvement is measured against a comparison using the same synthetic constraints, not announced as a real-event result.

# Decisions supported by the evidence

The Berre Lagoon episode is a credible choice for an immediate-response demonstration because it combines property damage, access restrictions, accommodation and constrained mobilisation. The strongest story is the transition from an individual report to a collective assistance plan under uncertainty. A completed settlement or a published CatNat order should not be invented to simplify the demo.

Before presenting exact real coverage, recover the applicable contract version and conditions. Before showing an official affected-area overlay, obtain a suitable authoritative geographic dataset. Before using a total-victims or total-loss headline, obtain a dated consolidated source. Until then those fields remain unknown rather than zero.
